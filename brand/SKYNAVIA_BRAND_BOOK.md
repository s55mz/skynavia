# SkyNavia Brand Book

## 1. Brand Strategy

### Brand Story
SkyNavia is a semiconductor brand for business clients who do not need more noise; they need orientation.

In the semiconductor category, most competitors communicate scale, inventory, and speed. That creates parity, not preference. SkyNavia wins by reframing the value proposition: it is not only a sourcing company, it is a decision interface for procurement teams. The brand exists to reduce ambiguity around hard-to-source parts, EOL risk, cross-reference options, and quality conditions.

This is why the identity is minimal. Minimalism here is not a visual trend. It is a business signal. If the interface is calm, the client infers the operation is disciplined. If the information hierarchy is clean, the client infers the sourcing logic is clean. The brand therefore turns clarity into trust.

### Strategic Positioning
- Category: Semiconductor sourcing and procurement
- Audience: Procurement managers, strategic buyers, trading firms, OEM/EMS partners, research organizations
- Positioning statement: `SkyNavia helps business clients make faster semiconductor procurement decisions by turning complex sourcing conditions into a clear, trusted proposal path.`
- Core differentiation: `Precision before persuasion`

### Brand Archetype
- Primary archetype: `Sage`
  Strategy: The Sage archetype fits a brand that earns trust through knowledge, structure, and rigor rather than charisma.
- Secondary archetype: `Explorer`
  Strategy: Semiconductor sourcing often requires route-finding across fragmented supply networks. Explorer introduces movement and range without making the brand theatrical.
- Internal shorthand: `The Navigator`
  Strategy: This proprietary shorthand fuses the two archetypes into a role that feels ownable for SkyNavia.

### Brand Principles
- `Clarity is a commercial advantage.`
  Strategy: B2B buyers move faster when the information order is obvious.
- `Precision is more persuasive than excitement.`
  Strategy: Semiconductor buyers respond to controlled confidence, not marketing flourish.
- `Every interaction should reduce uncertainty.`
  Strategy: The brand should make the next decision easier at each step.

## 2. Voice Matrix

| Dimension | How SkyNavia Sounds | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Authority | Specific, measured, proof-led | B2B semiconductor clients evaluate competence through detail |
| Warmth | Calm, helpful, direct | The brand should lower friction without sounding soft |
| Pace | Efficient, edited, non-repetitive | Procurement communication should respect time pressure |
| Vocabulary | Technical when needed, plain by default | Signals expertise without forcing jargon |
| Tone Risk to Avoid | hype, vague superlatives, startup clichés | These weaken trust in high-value sourcing contexts |

### Voice Do / Don't
- Do: `We can review alternate parts based on grade, lead time, and traceability.`
- Don't: `We provide world-class innovative solutions for all your sourcing needs.`
- Do: `Share the part number, required quantity, and timing. We'll structure the options.`
- Don't: `Contact us today to unlock the future.`

## 3. Messaging Hierarchy

### Level 1: Core Promise
`Semiconductor procurement, made easier to decide.`

Strategy:
The line avoids saying “easy sourcing,” which is often not believable in this market. Instead, it promises a better decision process, which is both credible and valuable.

### Level 2: Functional Value
- Hard-to-source semiconductor support
- EOL and continuity planning
- Alternate and cross-reference proposals
- Quality and traceability review

Strategy:
These messages translate the promise into concrete buying scenarios.

### Level 3: Proof Layer
- Structured intake of part, quantity, timing, and quality constraints
- Parallel search and verification workflow
- Proposal outputs that combine quote, risk, and alternates

Strategy:
Proof should show process discipline, not generic trust badges.

### Level 4: CTA Language
- Primary CTA: `Request Quote`
- Secondary CTA: `View Capabilities`
- Tertiary CTA: `Share Part Number`

Strategy:
CTA language is transactional and precise. It never sounds promotional.

## 4. Logo Directions

### Direction 1: North Frame
File: [`logo-01-north-frame.svg`](/Users/soramizukuki/projects/skynavia/brand/logo-01-north-frame.svg)

Concept:
A square frame represents system control and technical rigor. A diagonal axis suggests direction, routing, and intelligent movement. A single blue node marks the point of decision.

This is the strongest option because it captures both procurement discipline and navigation in one compact form.

- Proposal decks
- Email signatures

- Symbol only
- Reverse version on black

Usage rules:
- Keep clear space equal to the width of the node on all sides
- Never rotate the symbol
- Never remove the node
- Minimum digital size: 20 px icon / 120 px lockup

### Direction 2: Signal Path

A chain of nodes and a routed line reference signal flow, supply path, and continuity between decision points.

Strategy:
This direction is more explicitly semiconductor-coded than Direction 1, but slightly more descriptive and less iconic.

Recommended use:

- Diagrams
- Technical collateral

Variations:
- Horizontal lockup
- Path icon only
- Single-color technical version

Usage rules:
- Always maintain consistent node size
- Path stroke should never be thinner than 1.25 px in digital use
- Do not add gradients or glows

### Direction 3: Monoline Wordmark
File: [`logo-03-monoline-wordmark.svg`](/Users/soramizukuki/projects/skynavia/brand/logo-03-monoline-wordmark.svg)

Concept:
A restrained wordmark supported by a monoline underscore and a directional square, creating a confident editorial identity.

Strategy:
This is the most premium and least technical option. It works best if SkyNavia wants to behave more like a strategic procurement consultancy than a sourcing operator.

Recommended use:
- Corporate stationery
- Board-facing documents
- Investor or partnership materials

Variations:
- Wordmark only
- Wordmark with underscore device
- Reverse wordmark

Usage rules:
- Do not condense or expand letter spacing manually
- Use generous whitespace
- Best on white or solid black only

### Recommended Direction
Choose `Direction 1: North Frame`.

Strategy:
It balances semiconductor precision with business elegance. It is minimal enough for a Pentagram-style identity system, but distinctive enough to own.

## 5. Color System

The palette is intentionally reduced. SkyNavia should feel exact, premium, and controlled. Too many colors would weaken that signal.

| Name | Hex | Pantone Suggestion | CMYK | RGB | Use | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Obsidian | `#0A0A0A` | Pantone Black 6 C | 60 / 40 / 40 / 100 | 10 / 10 / 10 | Primary text, key surfaces | Deep black communicates seriousness and precision |
| Paper White | `#FFFFFF` | Paper / No Ink | 0 / 0 / 0 / 0 | 255 / 255 / 255 | Background | Pure white amplifies hierarchy and gives the brand editorial confidence |
| Circuit Gray | `#4D4D4D` | Pantone Cool Gray 11 C | 0 / 0 / 0 / 70 | 77 / 77 / 77 | Secondary text | Keeps contrast high without softening the identity |
| Trace Gray | `#E6E6E1` | Pantone 427 C | 2 / 1 / 3 / 10 | 230 / 230 / 225 | Rules, dividers | Light rules structure large white areas without visual clutter |
| Route Blue | `#1F4FFF` | Pantone 2728 C | 88 / 68 / 0 / 0 | 31 / 79 / 255 | Accent, node, highlight | A single electric blue introduces “signal” and “navigation” without becoming decorative |
| Route Blue Soft | `#EAF0FF` | Pantone 656 C | 8 / 5 / 0 / 0 | 234 / 240 / 255 | Subtle highlight planes | Used sparingly to support diagrams and interaction states |

Color rules:
- White should dominate
- Black is the default surface for emphasis
- Blue is a signal color, not a decorative brand wash
- No gradients
- No purple, teal, or multi-accent systems

## 6. Typography

### Primary Typeface
- `Inter`
- Roles: headlines, body copy, UI

Strategy:
Inter is neutral, highly legible, and commercially disciplined. It supports the “decision interface” positioning without bringing fashion-brand softness.

### Secondary Typeface
- `IBM Plex Mono`
- Roles: labels, metadata, technical references, part numbers

Strategy:
Mono should not be decorative. It introduces a technical register and helps coded information look intentional.

### Typographic Hierarchy
- Hero H1: Inter 800, tight tracking
- Section titles: Inter 800
- Body: Inter 400/500
- Labels: IBM Plex Mono 400/500, uppercase

Rules:
- No centered paragraphs longer than one sentence
- No ultra-light weights
- No more than two font families in any application
- Use mono only for metadata or coded content

## 7. Imagery Style

### Direction
- White or neutral backgrounds
- Macro details of wafers, dies, traces, test environments
- Precise crop geometry
- Low-emotion, high-detail compositions

### Avoid
- Generic handshake photos
- Blue abstract tech backgrounds
- Fake 3D chip renders
- People pointing at dashboards

Strategy:
The imagery should behave like evidence, not decoration. Procurement buyers trust precision photography and diagrammatic composition more than broad “innovation” visuals.

## 8. Graphic Language

### Devices
- Thin rule system
- Framed panels
- Data labels in mono
- Single-node blue markers
- Grids and procurement-flow diagrams

Strategy:
These devices make the brand feel systemized. They also allow the identity to scale across decks, web, and documentation without relying on illustration style.

## 9. Brand Applications

### Website
- Large white space
- Split-screen hero with functional intake panel
- Sparse color
- Decision-flow content before promotional claims

Strategy:
The website should feel like a procurement tool, not a campaign page.

### Quote Sheet
- White background
- Black headline
- Mono metadata blocks
- Blue used only for next-step emphasis

Strategy:
The quote sheet becomes a brand object, not just an operational document.

### Business Card
- Front: symbol + wordmark only
- Back: contact details in mono

Strategy:
This keeps the card quiet and memorable.

### Email Signature
- Wordmark
- Name / title
- Direct phone / email
- Thin divider

Strategy:
The signature should feel like a continuation of the interface.

### LinkedIn Banner
- One line promise
- One directional rule
- Symbol in corner

Strategy:
Social presence should reinforce clarity, not become louder than the main brand.

### Trade Show Wall
- Massive headline
- One proof statement
- One process diagram

Strategy:
From distance, the brand must still read as calm and precise.

## 10. Usage Rules

### Always
- Use white as the dominant field
- Maintain strong text contrast
- Use only one accent color in any composition
- Keep layouts left-aligned by default

### Never
- Use gradients
- Use bevels, glows, or glass effects
- Over-illustrate semiconductor concepts
- Turn blue into a background wash

Strategy:
The “never” rules are as important as the “always” rules because restraint is central to the identity.

## 12. Final Recommendation

SkyNavia should behave less like a “tech brand” and more like a precision instrument.

That is why the identity is reduced, black-and-white dominant, strongly typographic, and diagrammatic rather than illustrative. In a market crowded with visual noise, the quietest brand often appears the most capable.
